Step-by-step instructions for Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari, and Opera.

What is WebGL and why does it matter?

WebGL (Web Graphics Library) lets your browser draw 3D graphics and visual effects directly on screen using your computer's graphics card.

Without it, certain websites, games, maps, and design tools simply won't work — or will look broken. Enabling it takes less than 2 minutes.

Google Chrome

Most popular browser · Windows, Mac, Linux

1

Open the Chrome flags page

In the address bar at the top of Chrome, type: chrome://flags and press Enter.

This opens a hidden settings page. Changing only what's described here is completely safe.

2

Search for "WebGL"

At the top of the flags page you'll see a box labelled "Search flags." Click it and type WebGL.

3

Enable WebGL Draft Extensions

Find the result called "WebGL Draft Extensions." Click the dropdown next to it (it likely says Default) and choose Enabled.

4

Turn on GPU acceleration

In the address bar, go to: chrome://settings/system

Make sure "Use graphics acceleration when available" is toggled On.

5

Relaunch Chrome

A blue "Relaunch" button will appear at the bottom of the screen. Click it — Chrome will restart and your tabs will reopen automatically.

✅ Verify WebGL is Working

  1. In a new tab, visit webglreport.com
  2. If you see a colourful spinning cube or a detailed report, WebGL is enabled
  3. You can also click "Check My Browser Now" at the top of this page

Still not working?

Chrome says "WebGL is blocked" or shows a warning ▼
Your graphics driver may be on Chrome's blocked list. Visit chrome://gpu and look at the top section. Items highlighted in red indicate a driver issue. Update your graphics driver from your PC manufacturer's website (NVIDIA, AMD, or Intel), then restart Chrome.
Hardware acceleration is unavailable ▼
Chrome can't access your graphics card. Try: (1) updating Chrome via the three-dot menu → Help → About Google Chrome, (2) updating your graphics driver from the manufacturer's site, (3) restarting your computer fully.
I'm on a work or school computer ▼
Some organisations disable GPU access via group policy. You may need to contact your IT department to request that WebGL be enabled, or try a personal device instead.

Mozilla Firefox

Privacy-focused browser · Windows, Mac, Linux

1

Open Firefox's configuration page

In the Firefox address bar, type: about:config and press Enter.

A warning page saying "Proceed with Caution" will appear. Click "Accept the Risk and Continue" — this is normal and safe for what we're changing.

2

Search for the WebGL setting

In the search box at the top, type: webgl.disabled

You should see exactly one result with that name.

3

Make sure it's set to "false"

Look at the value on the right. If it says true, click the toggle button (double-arrow icon) at the far right to change it to false. If it already says false, WebGL is already enabled.

4

Enable hardware acceleration

Click the three-line menu (☰) in the top-right → Settings → scroll to Performance.

Make sure "Use hardware acceleration when available" is ticked.

5

Restart Firefox

Close all Firefox windows completely, then reopen the browser. Your changes are now active.

✅ Verify WebGL is Working

  1. In Firefox, type about:support in the address bar and press Enter
  2. Scroll down to the "Graphics" section
  3. Find WebGL Renderer — if it shows your graphics card name (e.g. "NVIDIA GeForce"), you're all set

Still not working?

WebGL Renderer shows "BlockList" or "Software" ▼
In about:config, search for webgl.force-enabled and toggle it to true. Also search for layers.acceleration.force-enabled and set it to true. Restart Firefox.
WebGL works but is very slow ▼
Firefox may be using software rendering instead of your GPU. Update your graphics drivers from the manufacturer's website (nvidia.com, amd.com, or intel.com), then restart Firefox. Confirm by checking the WebGL Renderer in about:support — it should show a real GPU name, not "Software Rasterizer."
I don't see webgl.disabled in the list ▼
If it doesn't appear, the preference doesn't exist yet — which means WebGL is using its default state (enabled). The issue is likely a browser extension. Try disabling all Firefox extensions and testing again. Some privacy extensions actively block WebGL.

Microsoft Edge

Built into Windows · Windows, Mac, Linux

1

Open the Edge flags page

In the Edge address bar, type: edge://flags and press Enter.

2

Search for WebGL

In the "Search flags" box at the top, type WebGL. Look for "WebGL Draft Extensions" in the results.

3

Set it to Enabled

Click the dropdown next to the result and select Enabled.

4

Turn on GPU acceleration

In the address bar, go to: edge://settings/system

Toggle on "Use graphics acceleration when available."

5

Restart Edge

Click the Restart button that appears at the bottom of the screen, or close and reopen Edge manually.

💻
Windows users: Edge is pre-installed and always up to date on Windows 10 and 11. Running Windows Update may also update Edge and your graphics drivers simultaneously.

✅ Verify WebGL is Working

  1. Type edge://gpu in the address bar and press Enter
  2. Scroll to "Graphics Feature Status"
  3. Find WebGL — it should say Hardware accelerated

Still not working?

WebGL shows "Software only, hardware acceleration unavailable" ▼
Edge can't access your GPU. Open Device Manager (press Windows key, type "Device Manager") → expand Display Adapters → right-click your GPU → Update driver. Alternatively, visit nvidia.com, amd.com, or intel.com to download the latest driver directly.
Edge is managed by my organisation and I can't change flags ▼
Work-managed Edge installations often block flags and GPU access via group policy. Contact your IT administrator and ask them to enable WebGL — specifically, they need to remove any "HardwareAccelerationModeEnabled = false" policy setting.

Apple Safari

Built into Mac · macOS and iOS

⚠️
Mac only: Safari on iPhone and iPad doesn't support enabling WebGL via settings — iOS controls it automatically. These steps apply to Safari on a Mac computer only.
1

Open Safari Preferences

In the menu bar at the top of your screen, click Safari → Settings (or Preferences on older macOS). You can also press ⌘ + , (Command + comma).

2

Enable the Develop menu

Click the Advanced tab (far right of the tabs row). At the very bottom, tick the checkbox: "Show features for web developers" (or "Show Develop menu in menu bar" on older macOS).

3

Open the new Develop menu

Close Preferences. In the menu bar at the top of your screen, you'll now see a new menu called Develop. Click it.

4

Enable WebGL in Experimental Features

In the Develop menu, hover over "Experimental Features." In the submenu that appears, make sure WebGL 2.0 and WebGL via Metal are ticked (have a checkmark next to them).

5

Restart Safari

Quit Safari completely using ⌘ + Q (Command + Q) and reopen it. WebGL is now active.

🍎
Keep macOS updated: Safari is updated alongside macOS. Go to System Settings → General → Software Update to ensure you're on the latest version, which often includes WebGL improvements.

Still not working?

I don't see "WebGL" in Experimental Features ▼
Your macOS or Safari version may be outdated. Update via System Settings → General → Software Update. On macOS 12 Monterey and later, WebGL 2.0 is enabled by default and won't appear in the list — this means it's already on and working.
The website asks me to use Chrome instead ▼
Some websites show this message based on browser detection alone, even when WebGL is working. You can usually dismiss the message and continue. If the site genuinely doesn't work in Safari, try Chrome or Firefox for that specific site.

Opera & Opera GX

Feature-rich browser · Windows, Mac, Linux

ℹ️
Opera and Opera GX are both built on the same engine as Chrome (Chromium), so the steps are nearly identical to Chrome.
1

Open the Opera flags page

In the Opera address bar, type: opera://flags and press Enter.

2

Search for WebGL

In the "Search flags" box, type WebGL. Find "WebGL Draft Extensions" in the results.

3

Enable it

Click the dropdown next to the result and select Enabled.

4

Enable GPU acceleration

Click the Opera logo (top-left "O" button) → Settings → scroll to the System section.

Toggle on "Use hardware acceleration when available."

5

Relaunch Opera

Click the Relaunch button that appears at the bottom of the screen. Opera will restart with your new settings.

✅ Verify WebGL is Working

  1. Type opera://gpu in the address bar and press Enter
  2. Look for WebGL in the Graphics Feature Status section
  3. It should say Hardware accelerated

Still not working?

Opera GX Power Saver is limiting my GPU ▼
Opera GX's Power Saver feature can restrict GPU usage. Click the GX Control panel on the left sidebar and check if Power Saver is active. Try disabling it temporarily and reload your WebGL page.
I've followed all steps and WebGL still doesn't work ▼
Try these final steps: (1) Update Opera via the O menu → Update & Recovery. (2) Update your graphics drivers. (3) Restart your computer. (4) On a laptop, check your GPU control panel (NVIDIA Control Panel or AMD Radeon Settings) and add Opera to the "High performance" GPU list — laptops often default to the weaker integrated GPU for browsers.
